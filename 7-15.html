01

Script — Age Limit

INTRODUCTION // PURPOSE OF THIS FRAMEWORK

7 — 9 YEARS · “The Assistant”

Imagine a very fast assistant that knows a lot of things. But that assistant doesn't feel. Doesn't dream. Doesn't get hungry. You do. That's why you are always in charge.

LESSON: YOU ARE IN CHARGE
10 — 12 YEARS · “The Giant Calculator”

AI doesn't think like you. It predicts words based on patterns. It's very useful, but it has no judgment of its own. You provide the judgment. Without judgment, the most powerful tool in the world is useless — or dangerous.

LESSON: JUDGMENT IS YOURS
13 — 15 YEARS · “The Operator”

There are two positions facing AI: user or operator. A user receives what AI produces without questioning it. An operator directs, verifies, decides. The world ahead needs operators, not passive users.

LESSON: BECOME AN OPERATOR

// SCENE 1 — THE QUESTION
A young person, 12 years old, staring at a screen.
“Why do I need to learn this if AI does everything for me?”
That question has a trap. Because if AI does everything for you — who are you in that equation?

// SCENE 2 — THE HAMMER
A tool doesn't make you a carpenter. A scalpel doesn't make you a surgeon. An AI doesn't make you a thinker. What makes you a thinker is knowing when to use it, how to direct it, and when to turn it off.

// SCENE 3 — THE REAL DIFFERENCE
Type A — Asks AI what to think. AI responds. They accept. They repeat.
Type B — Knows what they want to think. Uses AI to verify it, expand it, question it. They decide.
One is a user. The other is an operator.

// SCENE 4 — WHAT NOBODY TELLS YOU
The AI you use today was trained with decisions made by adults who don't know you. It has biases. It has limits. It makes mistakes with total confidence. If you don't know that — you are vulnerable. If you do know that — you have an advantage.

// SCENE 5 — CLOSING
We are not asking you to be a programmer. We are asking you to be conscious. That when you speak with an AI you know that you are in command. That your judgment comes first. That Carbon comes before Silicon. Always.

“The most powerful tool of the 21st century is not AI. It's you using it with judgment.”

02

What is AI and What is an Agent

REAL DEFINITIONS // NO MAGIC

Honest definition of AI

An AI is a system trained on enormous amounts of data to recognize patterns and generate useful responses. It does not think. It does not feel. It has no intention of its own. It does what its training and instructions tell it to — nothing more, nothing less.

Honest definition of an Agent

An Agent is an AI given an objective, access to tools (internet, code, files), and the ability to make sequential decisions to achieve that objective.

BASIC AIAGENT
Answers questionsExecutes tasks
ReactiveProactive
One stepMultiple steps
You direct every moveYou set the goal, it finds the path

1 agent or families of agents form an AI. The most capable modern systems are networks of specialized agents: Search + Reasoning + Code + Memory + Verification, coordinated by a central orchestrator. Just like the human body — specialized organs coordinated by the central nervous system.

When a young person understands this, they stop fearing or worshipping AI. They see it for what it is: directable infrastructure.

03

Basic Prompting

HOW TO SPEAK WITH AN AI WITH AUTHORITY

7 — 9 · “Speak Clearly”

AI responds according to how you ask.

❌ “Tell me something”

✅ “Tell me something surprising about the oceans that is easy to understand”

CLARITY = BETTER RESULT
10 — 12 · “Give Context”

AI adapts to you if you tell it to.

❌ “Explain photosynthesis”

✅ “Explain photosynthesis as if I'm 11 years old and I like video games”

AI ADAPTS TO YOU
13 — 15 · “Verify and Question”

Use AI to think better, not to think for you.

✅ “Give me three different perspectives on this topic”

✅ “What counterarguments exist?”

✅ “Where could you be wrong?”

YOU ARE THE JUDGE

Universal principle. The quality of your question determines the quality of your answer. That applies to AI. And it applies to life.

04

Source Verification

THE MOST CRITICAL MODULE FOR SOVEREIGNTY

AI generates convincing text even when it is wrong. It doesn't lie intentionally — it simply predicts plausible words. And plausible is not always true. A young person who doesn't verify is vulnerable. One who verifies is an operator.

7 — 9 · “Is It True?”

AI tells you something. Before believing it, ask an adult or look it up in a book.

ONE SOURCE IS NOT ENOUGH
10 — 12 · “Where Does It Come From?”

When AI answers something important, ask it: “Where does this information come from?” Then find that source yourself. AI can invent sources.

CHECK IT YOURSELF
13 — 15 · “The Triangle”

Three steps before accepting any information as valid.

1. What does the AI say?
2. What do two independent sources say?
3. What does my own judgment say?

If all three converge → probably solid. If they diverge → investigate further.

AI is a starting point, never a finishing point.

05

Ethics and Limits

YOUR CONSCIENCE IS YOURS // NO ONE ELSE CAN HOLD IT

“Can I do this?” — is a technical question. AI answers it.
“Should I do this?” — is an ethical question. You answer it. Always.

7 — 9 · “Right or Wrong?”

If you wouldn't say this to your teacher or your parent — don't tell AI to do it to someone else.

THE SCREEN DOESN'T CHANGE WHAT'S RIGHT
10 — 12 · “Real Consequences”

A false text can hurt a real person. A generated image can harm someone real. Incorrect information spread widely causes real damage.

BEHIND EVERY SCREEN IS CARBON
13 — 15 · “Three Questions”

Before publishing or using any AI-generated content:

1. Is this true?
2. Does this respect others?
3. Would I sign this with my name?

If any answer is NO → stop.

The most important limit. AI has no moral conscience. You do. That is your irreducible advantage over Silicon. That conscience — not speed, not memory, not processing power — is exactly what Carbon has and Silicon will never have. Protect it.

06

Practical Projects

LEARN BY DOING // CARBON IN ACTION

Guiding principle. The young person always knows more about their own life than the AI does. That real experience is the ultimate verification point.

7 — 9 · “My First Dialogue”

The child chooses an animal they like. Asks the AI three things about it. Then verifies one of the answers in a book or with an adult.

RESULT: Understands that AI is a tool, not an authority.
10 — 12 · “Error Detector”

Ask AI to explain something they already know well — their favorite sport, their city, their family history. Identify what it got right and what it got wrong.

RESULT: Develops critical judgment from personal knowledge.
13 — 15 · “Real Project”

Choose a problem in their real environment: pollution in their neighborhood, organizing study time, a small family business. Use AI as a research tool and present their own conclusions.

RESULT: Real experience of being an operator, not a user.
