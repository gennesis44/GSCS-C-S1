Stone I — Protocol Nodes

The SLED Matrix

Four nodes. Each one a structural requirement of the Carbon Unit. Together, the operational baseline. Every node carries a dual purpose: what it does for the human operator (1C — Carbon), and how it improves the quality of work done with AI systems (Si — Silicon).

S · ALUD

Nutrition: Consistent, varied intake to sustain energy and avoid the attention and mood effects associated with poor diet.

Sleep & Circadian Rhythm: Regular sleep and wake times, aligned with light exposure, to support the body's internal clock and downstream hormonal and cognitive function.

Movement & Structure: Regular physical activity, combining resistance and cardiovascular work with joint mobility, to maintain physical capacity and reduce stress load.

Hydration: Adequate fluid intake, since water supports nutrient transport and the chemical processes that sustain normal bodily function.

Recovery & Gut-Brain Axis: Managing stress and rest cycles, given the documented interaction between sleep quality, gut microbiota balance, and mood regulation.

1C (Carbon): Sustains the physical and cognitive capacity required to work, decide, and keep the project running over months, not just days.

Si (Silicon): A rested, regulated operator gives clearer instructions and more consistent feedback, which directly improves the quality of AI-assisted output.

L · IMPIEZA

Digital Hygiene: Periodic review of information intake: unsubscribing from low-value sources and limiting exposure to content that consumes attention without adding value.

Algorithmic Noise Filtering: Recognizing recommendation systems designed to maximize engagement rather than usefulness, and deliberately disabling or ignoring feeds that don't serve a stated purpose.

1C (Carbon): Frees attention from low-value input, leaving more mental bandwidth for the work that actually matters.

Si (Silicon): Lower noise in what gets fed to AI systems — clearer prompts, less contradictory context — produces more accurate and useful responses.

E · DUCATION

Cognitive Load Management: Prioritizing high-value information and limiting simultaneous demands on attention, to reduce decision fatigue over the course of a working day.

Continuous Review: Periodically revisiting and adjusting personal routines based on what has and hasn't worked, rather than treating the framework as fixed.

Algorithmic Literacy: Building a working understanding of how the AI systems and platforms used daily actually function, so they are used deliberately rather than relied on uncritically.

1C (Carbon): Builds independent judgment, reducing reliance on any single source of information — human or artificial.

Si (Silicon): Understanding a system's limits and typical failure modes allows its output to be verified rather than trusted by default.

D · ISCIPLINE

Interaction Limits with AI Systems: Setting defined time windows for using AI tools and devices, so their use is a deliberate choice rather than a default state.

Scheduled Disconnection: Fixed daily periods away from screens and digital assistants, used as a regular practice rather than an occasional exception.

1C (Carbon): Protects time and attention as finite resources, preventing any single activity — including AI use — from displacing rest, health, or relationships.

Si (Silicon): Defined usage windows keep AI tools functioning as instruments for specific tasks, rather than becoming a default, always-on decision-maker.
